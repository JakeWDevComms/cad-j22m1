Our Proposals

Caddick are pleased to bring forward proposals for a sustainable logistics and employment hub which would provide and support more than 1,000 jobs once operational, as well as creating opportunities for new and existing local businesses.

High-Quality Industrial Units

The submitted scheme provides around 64,800 sq m of high-quality industrial and logistics floorspace across six units, creating a new employment hub to support economic growth in the region. The mix of unit sizes is intended to provide opportunities for a range of occupiers, including growing local businesses.

The site would support more than 1,000 jobs once operational, generate an estimated £52.1m in annual GVA and around £2.2m per year in business rates.

Sustainable in construction and operation

Sustainability remains at the heart of the proposals, with a target of BREEAM Excellent and sustainable building practices integrated throughout construction and operation.

The proposals include EV charging facilities, air-source heat pumps, energy-efficient systems and PV-ready roofs with provision for battery storage.

Landscape, ecology and drainage

The proposals include a substantial landscape framework with around 17,000 new trees, woodland, hedgerow planting and habitat creation. National Forest planting requirements are met and key landscape features would be retained and strengthened where possible.

Key views from Altar Stones towards Billa Barra Hill and Bardon Hill would remain, with screening increasing as planting matures.

The proposals include habitat retention and creation, new wildlife corridors, long-term management and Biodiversity Net Gain.

Eight attenuation ponds would manage surface water and keep discharge below greenfield rates for the design storm plus climate allowance, reducing current run-off by 57% in the western catchment and 20% in the east.

Traffic and Access

The site benefits from direct access to the M1, A50 and wider Strategic Road Network.

Updated junction modelling shows M1 Junction 22 is forecast to operate within theoretical capacity in all assessed morning peak scenarios, with all approaches within desirable capacity in the assessed evening peak scenarios. The modelling forecasts only small increases in delays and queues, with around 79% of site traffic expected to route to the M1 away from local roads.

The proposals include an upgrade to the Whitwick Road / A50 junction to DMRB standard, a new signalised toucan crossing on the A50 and improved pedestrian and cycle connections.

Current positionNational Highways has issued a formal no-objection response. LCC Highways has also raised no objection.

Noise, lighting and air quality

Noise assessments show that development noise would be below 35dB, compared with an existing M1 background noise level of 55–63dB. No upward lighting is proposed and no likely significant effects on air quality have been identified.

Current positionNo objection from Environmental Health.

Heritage and archaeology

No listed buildings, scheduled monuments or conservation area are located on the site. Geophysical survey work has been undertaken and a 4% trial-trenching scope has been agreed.

Current positionScope agreed with the County Archaeology Officer.
