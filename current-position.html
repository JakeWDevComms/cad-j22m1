Current Position

Planning application 25/00931/FUL is awaiting determination by Hinckley & Bosworth Borough Council.

Planning policy position

The emerging Regulation 19 Local Plan allocates the site as MARK004 under Policy PS17 for strategic employment.

HBBC's Employment Land Review identifies a need for up to 65.7ha of B2/B8 space, while the Leicester & Leicestershire Strategic Distribution Study identifies a residual need of 168,109 sqm at Junction 22 on top of existing commitments.

The Markfield Neighbourhood Plan became out of date on 10 September 2026. The scheme continues to respond to its relevant countryside, landscape and habitat policies.

Application

25/00931/FUL

Full planning application submitted September 2025.

Current status: awaiting determination.

Response to points raised

The latest technical position

Traffic & highways

79% of site traffic routes to the M1. Traffic modelling shows the network can accommodate the development, including the Whitwick Road / A50 junction upgrade and new toucan crossing.

No objection: LCC Highways & National Highways

Landscape

Around 17,000 new trees are proposed. Key views from Altar Stones towards Billa Barra Hill and Bardon Hill would remain, with screening increasing as planting matures.

No major adverse impacts: HBBC Landscape

Flooding & drainage

Eight attenuation ponds would keep discharge below greenfield rates, reducing current run-off by 57% in the western catchment and 20% in the east.

No objection: LLFA, Severn Trent & HBBC Drainage

Noise, light & air

Development noise is assessed below 35dB against the existing M1 background of 55-63dB. No upward lighting is proposed and no likely significant air-quality effects are identified.

No objection: Environmental Health

Ecology

Biodiversity Net Gain would be secured through long-term habitat and hedgerow management, with wildlife corridors retained and created.

No objection: LCC Ecology, Natural England & HBBC Tree Officer

Heritage

No listed buildings, scheduled monuments or conservation area are located on the site. Geophysics has been undertaken and 4% trial trenching agreed.

Scope agreed: County Archaeology Officer

Local benefits

More than 1,000 operational jobs, 368 construction jobs, £52.1m estimated annual GVA and £7.9m social value, alongside training and local procurement.

Economic benefits

Consultation

2,262 consultation website views, 520 notification letters and direct engagement with the four closest properties. 375 households commented on the Planning Portal.
