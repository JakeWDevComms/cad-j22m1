Planning policy position

Allocated for strategic employment

The emerging Regulation 19 Local Plan allocates the site as MARK004 under Policy PS17 for strategic employment, reflecting an identified need at Junction 22.

The proposals are in line with the emerging and adopted Hinckley & Bosworth Local Plan when read as a whole. The Markfield Neighbourhood Plan became out of date on 10 September 2026, but the scheme continues to respond to its countryside, landscape and habitat policies.

Response to points raised

The latest technical position

The following summarises the position set out in the September 2026 Member Update Briefing.

Traffic & highways

79%
of site traffic routes to the M1

Traffic modelling shows the network is sufficient to accommodate the development. The Whitwick Road / A50 junction would be upgraded to DMRB standard, with a new toucan crossing on the A50.

No objection: LCC Highways & National Highways

Landscape

17,000
new trees across the site

National Forest planting requirements are met. From Altar Stones, key views towards Billa Barra Hill and Bardon Hill would remain, with the site increasingly screened as planting matures.

No major adverse impacts: HBBC Landscape

Flooding & drainage

57%
western catchment run-off reduction

Eight attenuation ponds would keep discharge below greenfield rates for the design storm plus climate allowance. The eastern catchment would see a 20% reduction.

No objection: LLFA, Severn Trent & HBBC Drainage

Noise, light & air

<35dB
development noise against 55-63dB existing M1 background

No upward lighting would protect dark skies around the site. No likely significant effects on air quality are identified.

No objection: Environmental Health Officer

Ecology

BNG
habitats and hedgerows secured through long-term management

Wildlife corridors are retained and created. No protected species were found other than badgers, with mitigation plans in place.

No objection: LCC Ecology, Natural England & HBBC Tree Officer

Heritage

4%
trial trenching agreed

No listed buildings, scheduled monuments or conservation area are located on the site. Archaeology has been tested through geophysics.

Scope agreed: County Archaeology Officer

Local benefits

£7.9m
social value, plus 368 construction jobs

Jobs in logistics and manufacturing are estimated to pay above the East Midlands median, with apprenticeships, training and local procurement also proposed.

+1,000 operational jobs and £52.1m estimated annual GVA

Consultation

2,262
consultation website views

520 notification letters were sent with leaflets, direct engagement took place with the four closest properties and 375 households commented on the Planning Portal.
