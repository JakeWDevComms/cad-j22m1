September 2026 update

From submitted proposal to current planning position

Caddick Developments submitted a full planning application to Hinckley & Bosworth Borough Council in September 2025. Since then, the application has progressed through technical assessment and consultation.

The latest position reflects the evidence now available on highways, landscape, drainage, ecology, heritage, noise, lighting and air quality, alongside the planning policy position and the identified need for strategic employment space at Junction 22.

Why Junction 22?

A strategic location for employment

The evidence base identifies Junction 22 as one of the Borough's most favourable locations for strategic employment development because of its direct access to the Strategic Road Network and strong labour accessibility.

04

Area of Opportunity

The Leicester & Leicestershire Strategic Distribution Study places J22 in Area of Opportunity 4 - the only area in the Borough rated green in its RAG assessment.

M1 / A50

Direct strategic access

The site sits immediately beside M1 Junction 22 and the A50, limiting the need for strategic traffic to travel through local settlements.

Response to points raised

What the latest evidence shows

The application has been assessed across the main matters raised during the determination process. The latest position is summarised below.

Traffic & highways

79%
of site traffic routes to the M1, away from local roads

Traffic modelling shows the network is sufficient to accommodate the development, including upgrades at Whitwick Road / A50 and a new toucan crossing.

No objection: LCC Highways & National Highways

Landscape

17,000
new trees; National Forest planting requirements met

Key views from Altar Stones towards Billa Barra Hill and Bardon Hill will remain, with the development increasingly screened as planting matures.

No major adverse impacts: HBBC Landscape

Flooding & drainage

57%
reduction in run-off in the western catchment; 20% in the east

Eight attenuation ponds will keep surface water discharge below greenfield rates for a 1-in-100-year storm plus a 40% climate allowance.

No objection: LLFA, Severn Trent & HBBC Drainage

Noise, light & air

<35dB
against 55-63dB existing M1 background

No upward lighting is proposed around the site, helping to protect dark skies. No likely significant effects on air quality have been identified.

No objection: Environmental Health Officer

Ecology

BNG
habitats and hedgerows secured through long-term management

Wildlife corridors would be retained and created. No protected species were found other than badgers, with mitigation plans in place.

No objection: LCC Ecology, Natural England & HBBC Tree Officer

Heritage

4%
trial trenching agreed with the County Archaeologist

There are no listed buildings, scheduled monuments or conservation areas on the site. Archaeology has been tested through geophysics.

Scope agreed: County Archaeology Officer

Local benefits

£7.9m
social value, plus 368 construction jobs

The scheme would support apprenticeships, training and local procurement, alongside jobs paying above the East Midlands median in key sectors.

Economic benefits identified through the application

Consultation

2,262
consultation website views

520 notification letters with leaflets were issued, alongside direct engagement with the four closest properties. 375 households commented on the Planning Portal.

Engagement recorded in the Statement of Community Involvement
